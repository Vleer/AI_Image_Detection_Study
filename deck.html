Welcome Screen
Participants first land here.
The welcome screen introduces the study as “a short study about how people judge digital images.” No mention of AI priming — participants are told only what they need to know to take part.
Figure 1 — Welcome screen (“Real or AI?”)

Informed Consent Screen
Two required checkboxes; Continue stays disabled until both are ticked.
Before any data is collected, participants confirm they are 18+ and that they agree to participate. Placeholder text reserves space for the full IRB Appendix B consent language.
Figure 2 — Informed consent screen
Slide 3 of 10 — Screen: Consent
Demographics Screen
Six required questions, all radio buttons except age.
Age (whole number 18–99), gender, education, AI-image exposure frequency, AI-image creation frequency, and confidence telling AI images from photographs. No free-text fields — answers cannot personally identify anyone.
Figure 3 — Demographics screen
Slide 4 of 10 — Screen: Demographics
Phase 1 — Baseline
“Phase 1” — no feedback given.
Participants judge 20 images on the 4-point scale (Definitely real → Definitely AI). They are told they will not find out whether they were right — this establishes their unprimed baseline accuracy.
Figure 4 — Phase 1 baseline trial
Slide 5 of 10 — Screen: Phase 1 intro + trial
Phase 2 — Training
“Phase 2 – you will now receive feedback”
Participants are told the next set works the same way, “with one difference: after each answer you will be told straight away whether you were right or wrong.” This is the priming block (20 images, AI rate assigned per condition).
Figure 5 — Phase 2 intro screen
Slide 6 of 10 — Screen: Phase 2 introduction
Phase 2 Trial (judgment)
Same 4-point judgment, feedback follows each answer.
The participant sees an image and answers. No color flashing, no full-screen overlay — only neutral text appears on the result area below the image after they respond.
Figure 6 — Phase 2 trial (feedback off until answer)
Slide 7 of 10 — Screen: Phase 2 judgment trial
Phase 2 — Result + Feedback
The moment that primes the participant.
Straight after the answer, a neutral result panel appears: “This image was AI-generated.” or “This image was real.” A Continue button advances to the next image. Self-paced, no timer, no animation — the priming happens through the repeated feedback, not through dramatic color.
Figure 7 — Phase 2 result + feedback screen
Slide 8 of 10 — Screen: Phase 2 result + feedback
Phase 3 — Post-Test
“Phase 3 – you will no longer receive feedback”
The final judgment block: 40 images, no feedback — identical in structure to Phase 1 so the only difference between pre- and post-test is what happened during Phase 2 training.

Phase 3 – you will no longer receive feedback

Last block: 40 more images, same task. The feedback is switched off again, so you will not find out whether you were right. Keep answering on first impression.

Figure 8 — Phase 3 introduction (app-styled card)
Slide 9 of 10 — Screen: Phase 3 introduction
Debrief Screen
The full reveal.
At the end, participants learn the study’s real purpose: whether brief feedback training shifts how readily someone calls an image “AI” — and whether that makes them worse, not better. They see their own scores per block, a completion code, and options to download (JSON/CSV) or delete their data. Nothing leaves the browser unless they choose.
